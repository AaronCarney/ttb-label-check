# What federal law requires on an alcohol label, and in what form

**Answer.** Every alcohol beverage label must carry the Government Health Warning, word for word as
27 CFR 16.21 gives it, with "GOVERNMENT WARNING" in capitals and bold and the rest not bold, on a
contrasting background, "separate and apart from all other information", at a minimum type size set
by container size (1, 2 or 3 mm) and a character density cap (40, 25 or 12 per inch). All three
beverage types must also show a brand name, a class/type designation, a name and address, and net
contents; imports also need a country of origin under CBP rules. Alcohol content is always required
for distilled spirits, required for wine only above 14% ABV (optional at or below 14% if the label
says "table" or "light" wine), and required for malt beverages only when alcohol comes from added
flavors or other nonbeverage ingredients. Only distilled spirits must put brand name, class/type
and alcohol content in one "field of vision". The alcohol tolerances (±0.3 points for spirits and
malt beverages; ±1 or ±1.5 points for wine) compare the label with the liquid, not the label with
the application form.

Sources read 2026-09-15, from eCFR (Title 27 shown current as of 2026-09-11) through its API,
`https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-09-11/title-27.xml?part=N`, and the Federal
Register API. The rule text itself is kept in [health-warning.md](../reference/health-warning.md)
and [label-elements.md](../reference/label-elements.md); this file records the findings and what
they mean for the app.

## Health warning (27 CFR part 16)

Source: <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-27/part-16>.

- Text, 16.21: "GOVERNMENT WARNING: (1) According to the Surgeon General, women should not drink
  alcoholic beverages during pregnancy because of the risk of birth defects. (2) Consumption of
  alcoholic beverages impairs your ability to drive a car or operate machinery, and may cause
  health problems." eCFR prints it as two paragraphs; joined with one space it is 283 ASCII
  characters.
- Placement, 16.21: on the brand label, a separate front label, or a back or side label,
  "separate and apart from all other information".
- Format, 16.22(a): readily legible, contrasting background; "GOVERNMENT WARNING" "in capital
  letters and in bold type"; "The remainder of the warning statement may not appear in bold type";
  not compressed so as to be illegible; maximum characters per inch 40 at 1 mm, 25 at 2 mm, 12 at
  3 mm.
- Type size, 16.22(b): at least 1 mm on containers of 237 mL (8 fl. oz.) or less; 2 mm above that
  up to 3 L (101 fl. oz.); 3 mm above 3 L.
- Affixing, 16.22(c): a label that is not part of the container must not come off without "thorough
  application of water or other solvents".
- Applies to all alcoholic beverages bottled or imported for sale in the United States on and after
  November 18, 1989, 16.20.
- Last amended by T.D. TTB-91, 76 FR 5477 (Feb. 1, 2011), per the source note in 16.22.
- TTB guidance adds that the warning "must appear as a continuous paragraph" (spirits page) or "a
  continuous statement" (malt page), and that other text may share the last sentence's line if
  separate and apart. The label application instructions say TTB "does not routinely review
  submitted labels" for type size, characters per inch or contrasting background. Quotes and URLs
  are in [health-warning.md](../reference/health-warning.md).
- TTB's Beverage Alcohol Manual for spirits and malt beverages is marked as not updated for the
  current parts 5 and 7, so this research uses the CFR, not the BAM.

## Mandatory elements by beverage type (27 CFR parts 4, 5, 7)

Sources: <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-27/part-4>, <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-27/part-5>,
<https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-27/part-7>.

| Element | Wine | Distilled spirits | Malt beverages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand name | Brand label, 4.32(a)(1) | Same field of vision, 5.63(a)(1) | Any label, 7.63(a)(1) |
| Class/type | Brand label, 4.32(a)(2) | Same field of vision, 5.63(a)(2) | Any label, 7.63(a)(2) |
| Alcohol content | Required above 14%; optional at or below 14% with "table"/"light" on the brand label, 4.36(a) | Required, same field of vision, 5.63(a)(3) | Required only with alcohol from added nonbeverage ingredients (not hops extract), 7.63(a)(3); else optional unless State law bars it, 7.65(a) |
| Name and address | Any label, 4.32(b)(1), 4.35 | Anywhere, 5.63(b)(1), 5.66–5.68 | Any label, 7.63(a)(4), 7.66–7.68 |
| Net contents | Any label, 4.32(b)(2), 4.37 | Anywhere, 5.63(b)(2), 5.70 | Any label, 7.63(a)(5), 7.70 |
| Country of origin | CBP, 19 CFR 102 and 134: 4.35(e) | Same, 5.69 | Same, 7.69 |
| Health warning | 16.21 | 16.21 | 16.21 |

Field of vision, 5.63(a): "a single side of a container (for a cylindrical container, a side is 40
percent of the circumference) where all of the pieces of information can be viewed simultaneously
without the need to turn the container". Parts 4 and 7 have no such rule for these elements.

Conditional statements (sulfites, FD&C Yellow No. 5, cochineal/carmine, aspartame; for spirits also
neutral spirits, coloring, age and State of distillation) exist in 4.32(c)–(e), 5.63(c) and
7.63(b). The brief does not list them, so they are noted, not detailed.

### Alcohol content: formats and tolerances

- **Spirits, 5.65.** Three formats: "Alcohol ____ percent by volume", "____ percent alcohol by
  volume", "Alcohol by volume ____ percent." Allowed abbreviations: "alc", "%", "/" for "by",
  "vol"; parentheses and periods optional. TTB's own examples: "40% alc/vol"; "Alc. 40 percent by
  vol."; "Alc 40% by vol"; "40% Alcohol by Volume." Proof is optional and must share the field of
  vision with the ABV, 5.65(b)(1)(i); proof is "twice the percentage of ethyl alcohol by volume",
  5.1. Tolerance ±0.3 points, 5.65(c).
- **Malt beverages, 7.65.** The same three formats and abbreviations; stated to the nearest 0.1 at
  0.5% or more; ±0.3 points, never below 0.5% if labeled 0.5% or more; "low/reduced alcohol" below
  2.5%; "non-alcoholic" and "alcohol free" have their own wording and no tolerance, 7.65(c)–(f).
- **Wine, 4.36.** "Alcohol __ % by volume" or a range "Alcohol __ % to __ % by volume", "or similar
  appropriate phrase"; abbreviations "alc." (alc) and "vol." (vol). Tolerance ±1 point above 14%,
  ±1.5 points at or below; a range may span at most 2 or 3 points; no statement may cross a class or
  tax-grade limit, 4.36(c).
- The brief's sample "45% Alc./Vol. (90 Proof)" fits 5.65: "%", "Alc.", "/" for "by", "Vol." and a
  proof in parentheses are all permitted, and 90 is twice 45.

### Net contents

- **Wine**: as written in the standard of fill (4.72 lists 25 metric sizes from 50 mL to 3 L, and even
  liters from 4 L); over 1 L in liters to the hundredth; under 1 L in "ml"; optional U.S. equivalent
  in fl. oz. to the tenth, 4.37.
- **Spirits**: "liter", "litre" or "L"; "ml.," "mL.," or "ML."; U.S. units and centiliters optional
  in the same field of vision, 5.70(a); 25 standards of fill from 50 mL to 3.75 L, 5.203 (last
  changed by T.D. TTB-200, 90 FR 1876, Jan. 10, 2025).
- **Malt beverages**: U.S. customary units required (fl. oz., pint, quart, gallon); metric only in
  addition, same field of vision, 7.70. No standards of fill.

### Name and address

- **Wine**: "bottled by"/"packed by" (or "imported by") + name + address; address is the post
  office address on the permit, street optional; trade name "identical with a name appearing on the
  basic permit", 4.35.
- **Spirits**: a function phrase ("bottled by", "distilled by", "imported by" …) + name + city and
  State; postal State abbreviations allowed; extra address detail optional, 5.66–5.68.
- **Malt beverages**: name + city and State; the function phrase optional for domestic bottlers;
  "imported by" for imports; postal abbreviations allowed, 7.66–7.68.

### Country of origin

The TTB parts only cross-refer to CBP. 19 CFR 134.45 (<https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/section-134.45>,
current as of 2026-09-11) requires the full English country name, but accepts abbreviations that
"unmistakably indicate the name of a country" and variant spellings such as "Brasil" and "Italie".
For wine, a foreign-language brand or producer name is allowed if "product of" precedes the country
name, 4.38(c).

### Presentation rules that affect checking

- Spirits and malt mandatory information may be in capitals, lower case or mixed case, 5.52(d),
  7.52(d). So "STONE'S THROW" and "Stone's Throw" are the same statement in law.
- Spirits and malt mandatory information, except brand names, must be "separate and apart from any
  additional information", but brief optional phrases may join the class/type ("premium vodka") or
  the name and address, 5.52(b), 7.52(b).
- Minimum type height 2 mm on most containers, 1 mm on small ones (wine 187 mL, spirits 200 mL, malt
  half pint), 4.38(b), 5.53, 7.53.

## Pending rules that could change this

Federal Register API, TTB proposed rules since June 2024, read 2026-09-15:

- Notice No. 237, "Alcohol Facts Statements…", 90 FR 6654 (Jan. 17, 2025),
  <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/17/2025-00957/alcohol-facts-statements-in-the-labeling-of-wines-distilled-spirits-and-malt-beverages>:
  would add an "Alcohol Facts" statement and make alcohol content mandatory for "certain types of
  malt beverages, beer, and wine that are not currently required"; proposed compliance 5 years after
  a final rule.
- Notice No. 238, major food allergen labeling,
  <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/17/2025-00955/major-food-allergen-labeling-for-wines-distilled-spirits-and-malt-beverages>.
- Comments on both closed 2025-08-15 (Notice No. 239). No final rule on either appeared in the
  Federal Register's TTB final rules through 2026-09-15. Neither touches the health warning.

## What this means for the app

1. The warning check is exact: word for word, "GOVERNMENT WARNING" capitalised, and bold where the
   image shows it. Type size and characters per inch need a physical scale a photo lacks, so the app
   should not claim to check them.
2. Which fields are required depends on beverage type, so the application data must say the type.
   A missing alcohol statement is a failure for spirits, and for wine over 14%, but not otherwise.
3. The field-of-vision rule applies only to spirits, and only if the app sees the whole side.
4. Country of origin applies to imports only.
