## 0. Authority basis and source-label legend

**Statutory bases**

- **FAA Act §105(e)–(f)** — 27 U.S.C. §205(e)–(f). Source of TTB labeling/advertising authority for "wine" (≥7% ABV under FAA Act), "distilled spirits," and "malt beverages."
- **Alcoholic Beverage Labeling Act of 1988 (ABLA)** — 27 U.S.C. §§213–219a. Source of the Health Warning Statement; implemented in 27 CFR Part 16.
- **Internal Revenue Code, Chapter 51** — 26 U.S.C. §§5001–5692. Source of excise-tax classification (parallel to, and partly disjoint from, FAA Act class/type).
- **Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C)** — 21 U.S.C. §§301 et seq. Governs alcohol products outside FAA Act scope (e.g., wine <7% ABV, beer that is not a "malt beverage" because it lacks malted barley + hops).
- **Organic Foods Production Act (OFPA), 1990** — 7 U.S.C. §§6501 et seq.; National Organic Program at 7 CFR Part 205.

**Regulatory bases**

- **27 CFR Part 1** — Basic Permit Requirements (FAA Act).
- **27 CFR Part 4** — Wine labeling. **Pre-modernization numbering still in force** as of April 2026; T.D. TTB-202 has *not* been issued (see §6).
- **27 CFR Part 5** — Distilled Spirits labeling. Current text reflects T.D. TTB-176 (eff. 3/11/2022) and T.D. TTB-199 (eff. 1/19/2025).
- **27 CFR Part 7** — Malt Beverages labeling. Current text reflects T.D. TTB-176 (eff. 3/11/2022).
- **27 CFR Part 9** — American Viticultural Areas (AVAs). Subpart C is the codified AVA list.
- **27 CFR Part 13** — Labeling Proceedings (rejection, qualification, appeal, revocation).
- **27 CFR Part 14** — *Reserved.* Despite Notice No. 176 proposing a consolidated advertising Part 14, T.D. TTB-176 left advertising **inside** Parts 5 (Subpart N) and 7 (Subpart N), and Part 4 (Subpart G) for wine. **Part 14 has not been promulgated as of 2026.**
- **27 CFR Part 16** — Health Warning Statement.
- **27 CFR Part 24** — Wine production / IRC tax-class labeling (cross-referenced for sub-7% wine and hard-cider tax-class).
- **21 CFR Part 101** — FDA food labeling (governs sub-7% wine and non-malt-beverage beers).
- **7 CFR Part 205** — USDA NOP (governs "organic" claims on alcohol).

**Operational guidance label legend (used inline below)**

- **CFR** — Binding regulation in the Code of Federal Regulations.
- **TD** — Treasury Decision (final rule), Federal Register.
- **BAM** — TTB Beverage Alcohol Manual (Distilled Spirits volume; Malt Beverages volume; the Wine BAM is the older 8/2018 chapter‑by‑chapter PDF set still posted to ttb.gov).
- **IC** — TTB Industry Circular.
- **RUL/PROC** — TTB Ruling or Procedure.
- **TTB-WEB** — TTB.gov public-guidance page (non-binding interpretation).
- **ALFD** — Alcohol Labeling and Formulation Division (TTB Boot Camp / training decks).

## 3. Government Warning specification (Q1.5) — verbatim text and full §16.22 rule set

### 3.1 Verbatim text (27 CFR §16.21)

> **GOVERNMENT WARNING:** (1) According to the Surgeon General, women should not drink alcoholic beverages during pregnancy because of the risk of birth defects. (2) Consumption of alcoholic beverages impairs your ability to drive a car or operate machinery, and may cause health problems.

The two words **"GOVERNMENT WARNING"** must appear in **capital letters and bold**; the remainder of the statement may **not** appear in bold (§16.22(a)(2)). The statement must appear **as a continuous statement** (TTB-WEB).

### 3.2 §16.22(a) Legibility rules

- (a)(1) Readily legible under ordinary conditions; on a contrasting background.
- (a)(2) "GOVERNMENT WARNING" — capitals and bold; remainder not bold.
- (a)(3) Letters may not be compressed in a manner that makes the warning not readily legible.

### 3.3 §16.22(a)(4) maximum-characters-per-inch table — VERBATIM from current eCFR

| Minimum required type size for warning statement | Maximum number of characters per inch |
|---|---|
| 1 millimeter | 40 |
| 2 millimeters | 25 |
| 3 millimeters | 12 |

(Source: eCFR `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-27/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-16/subpart-C/section-16.22`, retrieved 2026; T.D. ATF-294 as amended by T.D. 372 and T.D. TTB-91.)

### 3.4 §16.22(b) Minimum type size by container size

| Container size | Minimum type size |
|---|---|
| ≤237 mL (8 fl oz) | 1 mm |
| >237 mL up to 3 L (101 fl oz) | 2 mm |
| >3 L | 3 mm |

### 3.5 §16.22(c) Affixation

> "Labels bearing the statement required by §16.21 which are not an integral part of the container shall be affixed to containers of alcoholic beverages in such manner that they cannot be removed without thorough application of water or other solvents."

### 3.6 Other-text-on-same-line rule (TTB-WEB)

The sulfite declaration (or other non-mandatory information) **may** appear on the same line as the last sentence of the warning provided it is "separate and apart" from the warning. Source: Malt Beverage Labeling: Health Warning Statement (TTB-WEB).

## 4. Beverage-class designation rules (Q1.6)

### 4.1 Distilled Spirits

#### 4.1.1 Historical 12 classes (pre-modernization §5.22; cited for legacy COLA corpus)

The pre-T.D. TTB-176 §5.22 enumerated 12 numbered Classes:

1. Neutral spirits or alcohol (with subtypes vodka, grain spirits)
2. Whisky (with subtypes including bourbon, rye, wheat, malt, rye malt, corn, "light," "blended," straight, Scotch, Irish, Canadian)
3. Gin
4. Brandy (with subtypes including Cognac, fruit brandy, applejack, pomace/marc, grappa, Pisco, Singani)
5. Blended applejack
6. Rum
7. Tequila
8. Cordials and liqueurs
9. Flavored brandy / flavored gin / flavored rum / flavored vodka / flavored whisky
10. Imitations
11. Geographical designations
12. Products without geographical designations but distinctive of a particular place

#### 4.1.2 Current post-modernization structure (Subpart I, §§5.141–5.166)

The current Subpart I retains the same conceptual classes but reorganizes them into discrete sections; T.D. TTB-176 also added formal classes for **agave spirits** and **diluted spirits**, codified standards for white/un-aged whisky, and (via T.D. TTB-187, 1/13/2023) added **Singani** as a brandy type. T.D. TTB-199 (eff. 1/19/2025) added **American single malt whisky** as a type within the whisky class (§5.143 Table 1, with a 5-year transition allowing pre-1/19/2025 standards through 1/19/2030).

#### 4.1.4 Age statements (§5.74)

- Whisky <4 yrs old (or blends containing such): mandatory age + percentage-of-types statement, unless "Bottled in Bond" (which has its own standard).
- For all other whiskies, age is optional; if used, must conform to the age-statement format (must understate or match true age).
- "Aged ___ in reused cooperage" required for U.S. whisky aged in reused barrels (other than corn whisky, light whisky, American single malt, straight American single malt).
- Rum, brandy, agave spirits: age optional except brandy not in oak ≥2 yrs requires an age statement.

#### 4.1.5 State of distillation (§5.66(f))

For whiskies defined in §5.143(c)(2)–(7) (bourbon, rye, wheat, malt, rye malt, corn, light) distilled in the U.S., the **state of distillation** must appear on the label adjacent to the class/type designation. (This was a post-modernization addition implementing prior policy.)

#### 4.1.6 Tennessee whiskey

TTB *recognizes* "Tennessee whiskey" as a designation but does **not impose the Lincoln County Process** at the federal level — the production requirement (charcoal-mellowing through sugar-maple charcoal prior to aging, with a Prichard's-grandfather exception) is imposed by **Tennessee Code Annotated §57-2-106 (2013)**. TTB's enforcement for "Tennessee whiskey" labels is to require compliance with **state law**: the COLA reviewer treats it as a distinctive product whose qualifying parameters are state-defined. Federally, NAFTA/USMCA recognize "Tennessee Whiskey" as "a straight Bourbon Whiskey authorized to be produced only in the State of Tennessee."

### 4.2 Wine (Part 4, Subpart C)

#### 4.2.1 Standards of identity (§4.21)

Six classes:

1. Grape wine (table/light wine ≤14%; sparkling; carbonated; dessert)
2. Sparkling grape wine (incl. champagne; "crackling," "petillant," "frizzante")
3. Carbonated grape wine
4. Citrus / fruit / agricultural wine ("apple wine," "cider," "perry," "mead," etc.)
5. Wine from other agricultural products (e.g., rice wine, sake)
6. Aperitif wine (vermouth)
7. Imitation, substandard, other-than-standard

#### 4.2.2 Varietal labeling (§4.23)

- Single-variety: ≥75% of wine from named variety; the entire 75% from the labeled appellation. *Vitis labrusca* and "too-strongly-flavored" varieties may use ≥51% with "Contains not less than 51% [variety]" disclosure.
- Multi-variety: 100% of wine from named varieties; percentages on label (±2% tolerance); ordered by predominance.
- Variety names must be on TTB's approved list (formerly Subpart J; now maintained on TTB-WEB).

#### 4.2.3 Appellation of origin (§4.25, Part 9)

Categories: country (≥75%), state or multi-state up to 3 (≥75%), county or multi-county up to 3 (≥75%), AVA (≥85%, fully finished within state(s) of AVA).

#### 4.2.4 Vintage (§4.27)

- AVA (or foreign equivalent) appellation: ≥95% of wine from grapes harvested that year.
- Non-AVA appellation: ≥85% from that year.
- Appellation of origin **must accompany** the vintage date; must be substantially as conspicuous.

#### 4.2.5 Estate Bottled (§4.26)

The term "Estate Bottled" requires **all** of:

1. Wine labeled with an AVA appellation;
2. Bottling winery located in that AVA;
3. 100% of grapes grown on land **owned or controlled** ("controlled" = ≥3-year written lease) by the winery within the AVA;
4. Continuous on-premises crush → ferment → finish → age → bottle (wine never leaves bonding premises).

No alternative "estate"-style term is allowed for combined growing+bottling.

#### 4.2.6 Generic, semi-generic, non-generic (§4.24)

- **Generic** (have lost geographic significance entirely, only by Administrator finding): Vermouth, Sake.
- **Semi-generic** (geographic but also a class/type designation, by Administrator finding): Angelica, Burgundy, Claret, Chablis, Champagne, Chianti, Malaga, Marsala, Madeira, Moselle, Port, Rhine Wine (syn. Hock), Sauterne, Haut Sauterne, Sherry, Tokay. (Plus Retsina, treated as semi-generic since 2006.)
- **Conditions for use of a semi-generic name on non-EU wine** (§4.24(b)): must include a direct-conjunction appellation of origin disclosing true place of origin (e.g., "California Champagne"), and product must conform to TTB standard or trade understanding.
- **2006 US-EU Wine Trade Agreement** (codified by Tax Relief and Health Care Act of 2006, §422; 26 U.S.C. §5388(c)): bans **new** uses of the 17 Annex II names (the 16 above plus Retsina) on non-EU wine; **grandfather clause** preserves use by anyone whose COLA or certificate of exemption was issued **before March 10, 2006**, but only on labels for the same brand name (and fanciful name, if any) — i.e., a brand new SKU under a new brand cannot pick up a semi-generic. EU origin wines bearing these terms are unaffected.
- **Non-generic, distinctive designations** (must be products of the named place — e.g., Bordeaux Rouge, Médoc, Pommard, Chambertin, Liebfraumilch, Lacryma Christi).

#### 4.2.7 Vineyard/orchard/farm/ranch names (§4.39(m))

Such a name on a wine label requires ≥95% of the wine from primary winemaking material grown on that named property (relaxed only as a permitted *trade name* in the bottling address per §4.33(b) post-T.D. TTB-158).

### 4.3 Malt Beverages (Part 7, Subpart I)

- **§7.142 base classes**: any malt beverage may be designated "malt beverage." For products ≥0.5% ABV conforming to trade understanding: "beer," "ale," "porter," "stout," "lager," "malt liquor," with permitted descriptors like "amber," "brown," "red," "golden," "dry," "export," "cream," "pale."
- **§7.143** — products <0.5% ABV: "malt beverage," "cereal beverage," or "near beer."
- **§7.144 modifiers**: "Half and Half," "Ice," "Black and Tan," "Wheat" (≥25% wheat malt), concentrated/reconstituted.
- **§7.145** (low-alcohol): see §7.65(d)–(f).
- **§7.146 geographic names**: distinctive types with geographic significance (Pilsen, India Pale Ale [generic], Munich/Münchner, Vienna, Dortmunder, etc.). "Pilsen/Pilsener/Pilsner" produced in the U.S. or Czech Republic does not need geographic qualifier. Otherwise: "type" or "American" or place-disclosing adjective required. "Bourbon ale" or "Chardonnay lager" are prohibited cross-commodity designations (§7.122).
- **§7.147 specialty products**: products fermented/flavored with non-traditional ingredients require a distinctive/fanciful name + statement of composition (which becomes the class designation). Products covered by **TTB Ruling 2015-1** (and expanded by **IC 2024-2**) using listed traditional ingredients (e.g., honey, fruits, spices) may use designations in accordance with trade understanding rather than full SOC.
- **§7.65 ABV disclosure rules**: see §1.3 above and §2.

## 5. Prohibited elements (Q1.3) — by class

### 5.1 Wine (§4.39 + §4.64 advertising parallel)

- False or untrue statements; statements that disparage a competitor; obscene/indecent representations.
- **§4.39(a)(7)**: any statement, design, device, or representation (other than ABV per §4.36) that creates the impression the wine **contains distilled spirits, is comparable to a distilled spirit, or has intoxicating qualities.** This is the long-standing federal ban on "fortified wine" callouts on labels.
- Names of distilled-spirits products in brand names or class/type designations of wine ("Manhattan," "Martini," "Daiquiri," "Cuba Libre," "Zombie," "Collins," "creme/cream menthe," "creme cacao") (§4.39(a)(9)).
- Trade name of a living person/private organization that creates a false endorsement impression (§4.39(a)(6)).
- Foreign quality terms ("Auslese," "Eiswein," "Trockenbeerenauslese," "Qualitätswein," "Kabinett") on American wine (§4.39(l)).
- Use of a varietal/semi-generic/distinctive name in a brand name on wine that does not meet §4.21 classes 1, 2, or 3 (§4.39(n)).
- Misleading age/maturity statements (§4.39(b)).
- Geographic-significance product names (§4.39(j)).
- "Ice wine" (or variation) on wine made from grapes frozen *post-harvest* without qualification (TTB Ruling 2002-7 amplifying ATF 78-4 and 82-4).

### 5.2 Distilled Spirits (Subparts F, G, H — §§5.81–5.132)

- Subpart F restricted: organic claims (§5.84); "barrel proof" (§5.87); "bottled in bond" (§5.88); multiple distillation claims (§5.89); Scotland-related terms (§5.90); "pure" (§5.91).
- Subpart G prohibited outright (§§5.101–5.103): false/untrue statements; obscene/indecent depictions.
- Subpart H prohibited if misleading (§§5.121 et seq.): misleading statements/representations; guarantees; reference to the U.S. government as endorser; cross-commodity confusions; living-person endorsements.
- Bourbon designation forbidden on whisky not distilled and aged in the U.S. (§5.143(c)).
- Geographic designations (Cognac, Armagnac, Calvados, Pisco, Tequila, Mezcal, Cachaça, etc.) restricted to true place of production unless qualified (§5.156).

### 5.3 Malt Beverages (Subparts F, G, H — §§7.81–7.132)

- Cross-commodity terms ("bourbon ale," "Chardonnay lager," "lager with whisky flavors") prohibited (§7.122).
- False, obscene, or indecent statements (§§7.101–7.103).
- Misleading guarantees, statements about composition, indications of intoxicating effects (§§7.121–7.132).
- "Strong" — as of T.D. TTB-158 (2020) the historic ban was removed; "strong" and "full-strength" are now permitted as truthful and non-misleading descriptors.

## 8. Allowable Revisions implications (Q1.9) — Form 5100.31 (rev 04/2023)

- **Form**: TTB F 5100.31, "Application For And Certification/Exemption Of Label/Bottle Approval," current revision **04/2023** (OMB control 1513-0020). The Allowable Revisions list is on **pages 3 and 4** of the form.
- **Current count**: **41 allowable-revision items** as of 2025–2026 (TTB IC 2021-1 expanded the list from 37 to 41; T.D. TTB-176 made conforming changes; the count of 41 is reflected on the form-current revision and on `ttb.gov/labeling/allowable-revisions`).
- **Items 38–41 added by IC 2021-1** (3/2021):
  - **38** — add/change/delete **opening instructions for boxed-wine containers** (graphics permitted)
  - **39** — add/change/delete **tap instructions for kegs** (wine and malt beverages)
  - **40** — add/change/delete **container manufacturer name and/or address** (wine, spirits, malt)
  - **41** — change the numerical **IBU (International Bitterness Units) or Original Gravity** statement on malt beverage labels
- **Items expanded by IC 2021-1**: **Item 36** (sustainable-farming/eco-friendly claims) now includes "Environmentally Conscious" and "Eco-Conscious"; combining-of-separate-approved-labels and color/cosmetic changes were also expanded.
- **Operational implication**: A change matching one of the 41 items (within the listed conditions) does **not** require a new COLA. **Critical caveat**: any revision must still comply with 27 CFR Parts 4, 5, 7, and 16 and any conditions listed in the Form 5100.31 "Comments" column. Examples of allowed changes:
  - Add/delete/change non-mandatory information, graphics, or "drink responsibly" text.
  - Update freshness ("Best By") or Consume-By date.
  - Change UPC.
  - Change ABV statement *consistent with the existing class/type designation*.
  - Update social media handle.
  - Tax-class sticker for hard cider (`Tax class 5041(b)(6)`).
- **Disallowed-without-COLA changes** (per TTB-WEB Allowable Revisions page):
  - New graphics/pictures/representations not specifically authorized.
  - New wording/phrases/text/certifications (other than serving instructions and similar specifically-authorized ones).
  - Change in brand name.
  - Change in class/type designation.

## 9. Cross-jurisdictional edge cases (Q1.10) — TTB / FDA / IRC boundaries

### 9.1 Wine ABV jurisdictional matrix

| ABV | FAA Act (TTB Part 4)? | IRC tax (Part 24)? | FDA (21 CFR Part 101)? |
|---|---|---|---|
| <0.5% | No | No (not "wine" under IRC) | Yes |
| 0.5% to <7% | **No** (FAA Act starts at 7%) | **Yes** — taxed under 26 U.S.C. §5041; labeled per 27 CFR §24.257 (kind, ABV, tax-class designation) | **Yes** — full FDA food labeling (Nutrition Facts panel, allergen statement under FALCPA, ingredient declaration). 27 CFR Part 16 health warning still applies because ≥0.5% triggers ABLA. |
| 7% to ≤24% | **Yes** — Part 4 applies | Yes | No (TTB labeling preempts where it applies) |
| >24% | No (reclassified) | Taxed as distilled spirits under §5001 | No |

### 9.2 Hard cider (cider/perry) — IRC §5041(b)(6) "hard cider" tax rate

**Eligibility (post-PATH-Act, eff. 1/1/2017):**
- Still wine (≤0.64 g CO₂/100 mL — 2× the original limit);
- Derived primarily from apples or pears or apple/pear juice concentrate + water;
- Contains **no fruit product or fruit flavoring other than apple or pear** (spices, honey, hops do **not** disqualify);
- ≥0.5% and **<8.5% ABV** (raised from <7%).

**Labeling**:
- ≥7% ABV → Part 4 COLA + designation that ID's the tax class (e.g., "hard cider," "apple wine," "perry").
- <7% ABV → exempt from Part 4 but subject to Part 24, Part 16, and FDA Part 101.
- All hard-cider products must bear "**Tax class 5041(b)(6)**" statement (min 1 mm/2 mm) since 1/1/2019 (per IC 2017-2 + delay rule).

### 9.3 Saké

- IRC §5052(a) defines "beer" to include "saké or similar products" — for excise-tax purposes, saké is **beer**, taxed under §5051.
- FAA Act §117(a) (27 U.S.C. §211(a)(6)–(7)): "wine" includes saké if ≥7% ABV; "malt beverage" requires malted barley + hops, which saké lacks.
- **Result**: saké ≥7% ABV is regulated as **wine for FAA Act labeling** (27 CFR Part 4), but as **beer for IRC tax** (Part 25). Saké between 0.5% and 7% ABV is "beer" for tax but **subject to FDA labeling** (because not a "malt beverage" under FAA Act and not "wine" under FAA Act <7%).

### 9.4 Hard seltzer / Flavored Malt Beverage (FMB)

- FMB containing alcohol from added flavorings and other added nonbeverage ingredients (other than hops extract): subject to §7.5 (≤49% of overall ABV from added flavorings; up to 1.5% ABV or 49%, whichever is less, for >6% ABV products); ABV statement is **mandatory** under §7.63(a)(3).
- Hard seltzer: typically classified as either a malt beverage (if brewed from malted barley + hops then flavored) or as a "beer not a malt beverage" (e.g., from sugar+yeast+flavor) which is taxed as beer (IRC §5052) but **not** subject to FAA Act labeling — subject to **FDA labeling** instead.
- Hard seltzers made from a wine base ≥7% are subject to Part 4; below 7%, FDA.

### 9.5 Wine >24% ABV

> "Products that would otherwise meet the definition of wine except that they contain more than 24 percent alcohol by volume are classified as distilled spirits and must be labeled in accordance with [Part 5]." — 27 CFR §4.6(b)

### 9.6 Saccharin warning

- TTB historically required a saccharin warning on alcohol labels keyed to former 21 CFR §101.11 and former FFDCA §403(o)/(p).
- **§403(p)** (retail-establishment saccharin notice) was repealed in **1996** by the Saccharin Notice Repeal Act (Pub. L. 104-180).
- **§403(o)** (food-misbranding-warning requirement) was repealed in **2000** by Pub. L. 106-554.
- 21 CFR §101.11 today is **a vending-machine calorie-disclosure regulation**, not a saccharin notice. The saccharin food-additive interim regulation at 21 CFR §180.37 remains, but no longer requires a consumer warning.
- **Practical**: a TTB validator should not require a saccharin warning. (See Open Items §12.)

## 10. Adjacent regulatory dependencies

### 10.1 27 CFR Part 1 (Basic Permit Requirements)

- A FAA-Act basic permit is required to engage as a **distiller**, **rectifier/processor**, **wine producer**, **wine blender**, **importer**, or **wholesaler** (§§1.20–1.22).
- The **trade name(s)** ("DBA") and the **legal entity name** must each be on the basic permit before a label may use either as a "bottled by"/"produced by"/"imported by" name. Form **TTB F 5100.24** captures Item 4 (operating name/DBA) and Item 5 (labeling trade names) — these populate downstream COLA validation.
- **Permits Online** ties permits to entities; **COLAs Online** (Item 8 of Form 5100.31) requires the registrant to enter their permit number, brewer's notice number, or DSP/BWC/TPWBH registry. The bottler/importer name+address on the label must be **identical** to a name registered to that permit. This is the operational hook for the validator's name/address validation (§4.35(d); §5.66(d); §7.66(c)).
- Brewers may operate without an FAA-Act basic permit (only a brewer's notice under 27 CFR Part 25) but must still register all trade names with TTB before labeling.

### 10.2 27 CFR Part 9 (American Viticultural Areas)

- Subpart A: definitions (§9.1).
- Subpart B: AVA petition process (§§9.11–9.14). To create or modify an AVA: petitioner submits evidence of name, evidence of boundaries, evidence of distinguishing features (climate, soil, elevation, geology), USGS-marked maps; TTB issues NPRM in Federal Register; public comment; TTB issues final rule (Treasury Decision) codifying the new AVA into Subpart C.
- Subpart C: codified AVA list (§§9.21 et seq.; section numbers extend through approximately §9.289 as of 2026).
- **Count as of late 2025**: TTB-WEB ("Established AVAs") shows **279 AVAs**; California holds 154. (Independent compilations note 277 as of September 2025; the TTB-maintained count rises as final rules issue.) Recent additions include Tryon Foothills (NC), Beverly (WA), Crystal Springs of Napa Valley, San Luis Rey, Upper Cumberland, Yucaipa Valley, Comptche, Contra Costa, etc.
- **Validator implication**: Maintain an AVA whitelist sourced from the eCFR Part 9 Subpart C section list (or scrape `ttb.gov/wine/established-avas`); refresh on each Treasury Decision establishing a new AVA. The validator's AVA list must be versioned to a date.

### 10.3 27 CFR Part 13 (Labeling Proceedings)

See §7.1 above.

### 10.4 21 CFR Part 101 (FDA labeling)

Applies when FAA Act does not (sub-7% wine; sub-0.5% products; non-malt-beverage beers). Key sub-sections:

- **§101.4** — designation of ingredients (descending order of predominance).
- **§101.9** — Nutrition Facts panel.
- **§101.22** — labeling of spices, flavorings, colorings.
- **§101.36** — dietary supplement nutrition labeling.
- **§101.100** — exemptions; **§101.100(a)(4)** is the **10 ppm sulfite trigger** — sulfites in any food (incl. sub-7% wine and beer-not-malt-beverage) at ≥10 ppm must be declared.
- **§101.11** — vending-machine calorie disclosure (NOT a saccharin notice; see §9.6).
- **§101.17** — food labeling warning and notice statements.
- **FALCPA** allergen labeling — 9 major allergens including sesame (added 2023).

### 10.5 7 CFR Part 205 (USDA NOP)

- Categories: **"100% Organic"** (USDA seal allowed); **"Organic"** (≥95% organic ingredients); **"Made With Organic ___"** (≥70% organic; cannot use USDA seal); **"Specific organic ingredients"** (<70%; ingredient-list only).
- **§205.605**: sulfur dioxide allowed only in wine labeled "**made with organic grapes**," with total sulfite concentration capped at 100 ppm. Wine labeled simply "**organic**" must not have **added** sulfites.
- **§205.680** appeal process; TTB defers to NOP/AMS for organic-claim eligibility; appeals route to USDA per 27 CFR §13.101.
- Cross-references: 27 CFR §4.41 (wine), §5.84 (spirits), §7.85 (malt beverages).

### 10.6 26 U.S.C. Chapter 51 (IRC excise taxes)

- **§5041(a)–(b)** — wine tax classes:
  - (b)(1) Still wine ≤16% ABV (post-CBMA; "still" includes mead and "low-alcohol-by-volume wine") — $1.07/wine gallon
  - (b)(2) Still wine >16% to ≤21% — $1.57
  - (b)(3) Still wine >21% to ≤24% — $3.15
  - (b)(4) Champagne and other sparkling wines — $3.40
  - (b)(5) Artificially carbonated wines — $3.30
  - (b)(6) Hard cider — $0.226
- **§5041(c)** — small-producer credit ($1/0.90/0.535 per wine gallon for first 30k/100k/620k; adjusted to $0.062/0.056/0.033 for hard cider).
- **§5041(g)** — "hard cider" definition (see §9.2).
- **§5041(h)** — mead and "low alcohol by volume wine" deemed still wine ≤16% for §§(a) and (b)(1) tax purposes.
- **§5051** — beer tax.
- **§5052(a)** — "beer" defined as "beer, ale, porter, stout, and other similar fermented beverages (including saké or similar products) of any name or description containing one-half of 1 percent or more of alcohol by volume, brewed or produced from malt, wholly or in part, or from any substitute therefor." Saké is **beer** for IRC tax purposes.
- **§5388(c)** — semi-generic name use restriction (post-2006 EU agreement; see §4.2).

## 12. Open items and notes for the researcher

1. **Saccharin warning historical text** — the prior pass flagged this. Confirmed: former 21 CFR §101.11 (saccharin retail-establishment notice) and FFDCA §403(o)/(p) requirements have been **repealed (1996, 2000)**. 21 CFR §101.11 today addresses vending-machine calorie disclosure, not saccharin. There is no current TTB saccharin labeling mandate; any "saccharin warning" in legacy COLA corpora is grandfathered or outdated. Validator should not flag absence of a saccharin warning.
2. **T.D. TTB-202 wine modernization** — confirmed **not issued** as of 4/2026. Wine modernization is in TTB's "Phase 3" but no NPRM beyond Notice No. 176 (2018) addressing wine specifically has been re-noticed. **Part 4 retains pre-modernization numbering**; validators should anchor on §§4.21–4.27, 4.32, 4.34, 4.35, 4.36, 4.37, 4.38, 4.39, 4.50, 4.62 etc. Risk: if T.D. TTB-202 issues during 2026, a renumbering remediation will be required (analogous to the §5.32→§5.63 / §7.22→§7.63 mapping done after T.D. TTB-176).
3. **Notice 237 (Alcohol Facts) and Notice 238 (Allergens)** — proposed only; 5-year compliance windows from any final rule. **Not currently mandatory.** A validator should not flag absence.
4. **§16.22(a)(4) CPI table** — confirmed verbatim from current eCFR (§3.3 above). Three rows: 1 mm → 40 cpi; 2 mm → 25 cpi; 3 mm → 12 cpi. **No interpolation values for type sizes between 1, 2, and 3 mm; the table is cardinal, not continuous.** Industry practice uses the next-larger row when type size falls between increments.
5. **§4.36(b) wine ABV tolerance text** — confirmed. The change to the 14% / 16% IRC tax-class line (Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, 2017; permanently extended 12/27/2020) **did not** alter §4.36 tolerances; TTB has explicitly stated that the FAA Act regulation continues to operate on the 14% line for FAA-Act ABV tolerances and class designations of "table wine." Verified via TTB-WEB Wine Labeling: Alcohol Content; FAQ/letter response.
6. **§7.65(c) malt beverage ABV tolerance** — confirmed: ±0.3 pp; floor of 0.5% ABV cannot be crossed; "low/reduced alcohol" cannot reach 2.5% regardless of tolerance.
7. **"Alc./Vol." abbreviation** — explicit regulatory permission is in **§4.36(b)(1)** proviso ("alc." (alc) and/or "vol." (vol)); "Alc./Vol." composite phrasing is permissible by direct application of this proviso. Same logic supports use of "Alc/Vol" with slash on malt beverages per §7.65(b)(4) ("alcohol and volume may be separated by a slash ('/') in lieu of the word 'by'").
8. **"ABV" abbreviation** — **not authorized**. TTB-WEB Malt Beverage Labeling: Alcohol Content explicitly says "the abbreviation 'ABV' is not allowed." Likewise "ABW."
9. **Lincoln County Process / Tennessee whiskey** — TTB does not enforce the LCP at the federal level; it requires compliance with **Tennessee state law (Tenn. Code Ann. §57-2-106)** for any "Tennessee whiskey" designation. Validator should treat "Tennessee whisky" as a state-defined distinctive product without imposing the LCP test directly; rely on the producer's state of distillation + the appearance of "Tennessee whiskey" as a designation.
10. **Saké treatment** — split jurisdiction: **wine** under FAA Act (≥7%); **beer** for IRC tax purposes (§5052). Validator must classify on **two axes** simultaneously: FAA-Act labeling pathway (Part 4) and IRC tax-class (§5051).
11. **Part 14 status** — Part 14 was proposed in Notice 176 (2018) for advertising consolidation but **not enacted** by T.D. TTB-176 or TTB-158. Advertising regs remain in **Subpart G of Part 4**, **Subpart N of Part 5**, and **Subpart N of Part 7**. Part 14 in the current CFR is Reserved/empty.
12. **AVA count** — TTB-WEB shows **279** as of late 2025; this is authoritative for whitelist construction. Subsequent TDs (e.g., Tryon Foothills, Beverly WA, Crystal Springs of Napa Valley, San Luis Rey) bring the running tally; validator should refresh AVA whitelist at least quarterly using `https://www.ttb.gov/regulated-commodities/beverage-alcohol/wine/established-avas` plus the eCFR Part 9 Subpart C section list.
13. **Allowable Revisions count** — confirmed **41** items as of 2025-2026. The 04/2023 revision of Form 5100.31 is the current paper revision; the corresponding TTB.gov page shows the 41-item list; this matches IC 2021-1 (which added items 38–41 and expanded items 36 and others). Note: the form revision date (04/2023) does not advance every time the underlying list changes; rely on the TTB.gov page as the authoritative current text.
14. **Cochineal/carmine** — TTB requires disclosure on labels for product **removed from bonding/customs custody on or after 4/16/2013**; older COLAs without the disclosure are grandfathered for product removed before that date but are not valid for new bottling.
15. **Rejection-reason taxonomy gap** — confirmed: there is **no machine-readable, TTB-published rejection-reason code list**. The 28-bin operational taxonomy in §7.3 is synthesized from BAM, ALFD Boot-Camp materials, the TTB-WEB Allowable Revisions page, the COLAs Online FAQ, TTB Distilled Spirits Formulas and Labels (Nov 2024), and industry compilations from Lehrman Beverage Law / Lindsey Zahn P.C. / getOllie / FIVE x 5 / Park Street / Nixon Peabody / Sovos ShipCompliant / CSA Compliance. Validator developers should treat this taxonomy as a **non-authoritative working set** and tag each bin with its underlying CFR citation as the binding reference.
16. **Net contents abbreviations** — Part 5 is explicit: "L" (or "litre"), "ml.," "mL.," or "ML." Part 4 (§4.37) is more permissive: "liter" / "litre" / abbreviations consistent with industry practice. The validator should accept all four ml capitalizations and "L" capitalized only (lowercase "l" is **not** in the regulation as an explicit abbreviation; safe to flag for human review).
17. **"Alc/Vol" without periods** — supported by the §4.36(b)(1) and §7.65(b)(3) provisos which expressly allow "(alc)" and "(vol)" without periods.
18. **SoC matching to formula** — for any spirit or wine requiring a TTB-approved formula (cordial, liqueur, flavored spirit, distilled spirits specialty, formulated wine), the SOC on the label must align with the formula's RESULTS-tab SOC. This is the #1 distilled-spirits return reason per TTB's Nov 2024 newsletter and is not a CFR rule per se — it is a corollary of §5.155 / §4.34(a) / §7.147.
